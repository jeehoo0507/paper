## Summary: How to Use This List

**Context.** We are writing a survey on *efficient knowledge distillation (KD) for vision*: methods that reduce the **training-time cost of the distillation process itself** (teacher forward passes, training data, training steps) for CNN/ViT image classification. This is different from "KD as a compression tool," which only reduces the student's inference cost.

| PART 1 | **How to write.** Chosen for writing quality, not topic or popularity. Use as structural models; do not cite as related work. | One central argument carried through the whole survey (#1, #12); one unified formulation that recasts all methods (#2, #4, #5, #6, #10, #12); framing the field as a sequence of paradigm shifts (#6); quantitative statistics on the literature itself (#9); practical guidance or recipes for practitioners (#7, #12); one decisive taxonomy axis with explicit rules for borderline methods (#11); a per-method summary table plus a self-critical retrospective (#8); taxonomy figure with section numbers, paper-count trend plot, and trade-off figure (#3).                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| PART 2 | **Prior KD surveys to cite and differentiate from.**                                                                          | #1–#5 do not treat the cost of the distillation process as a taxonomy axis or as a column in any comparison table. **#6 is the exception and the closest prior work in framing**: a cost-accounting survey of distillation, but only for on-policy distillation of language models (single-author preprint, not peer-reviewed, no new experiments). What it does: organizes efficiency evidence into six tracks (data, rollout, teacher supervision, optimization, systems, deployment amortization); defines training cost as preparation plus per-round generation, scoring, update and coordination; keeps an "evidence ledger" recording the workload, baseline, measurement boundary and quality condition behind each reported saving; and proposes an evaluation protocol (fixed workload / fixed budget and target quality / matched deployment). Cite it and state the difference (vision, supervised/offline KD, our own measurements). Borrow with attribution: the cost split into preparation + per-round terms, cost-to-reach-a-target-quality as the comparison quantity, the three tests of an efficiency claim (resource / quality / mechanism identification), the rule that a missing cost field is "not reported" and never zero, and the minimum reporting template. #1 (Gou) is the standard taxonomy; #2 (Wang & Yoon) is the vision-specific baseline; #3 (Mansourian) is the latest broad survey and explicitly avoids quantitative comparison; #4 (Xu) is the main LLM KD survey and lists distillation cost only as a brief open problem; #5 (LM-KD) is the closest competitor (discusses compute allocation and teacher caching, but is language-only).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| PART 3 | **Evidence and tools for our own argument.**                                                                                  | Cost formulation: #4 (Treviso, Cost ∝ E·D·H) and #5 (Bartoldson, total time = iterations × per-iteration time; we add a teacher-forward term). Proof that methods are not comparable: #1 (Blalock, meta-analysis plus controlled benchmark). Benchmark and comparison-table format: #3 (PEFT for Vision), #2 (Efficient Reasoning, separates training cost from inference cost). Closest gap: #6 (Efficient ViT; its efficiency metric counts only student inference cost). Scope definition by formula: #7. One-line supporting citations: #8 (KD process is resource-intensive), #9 (vision efficiency surveys treat KD only as inference compression), #10 (teacher cost is a known issue for diffusion distillation). |
| PART 4 | **AIM Lab Korean papers** (listed in `survey- ko.md`, which has its own summary). | #4 is essential: teacher-output caching for detection, with training-time and storage columns (storage is a separate cost term). #5 supports the claim that KD methods differ up to 4x in per-step training cost. #1–#3 are not topic-relevant. |
